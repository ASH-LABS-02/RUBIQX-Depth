PART 1 — HOW YOU WILL BE JUDGED
================================================================================

From the problem statement and the organisers' FAQ (GitHub issue #1):

1. Final test images are Cartosat-2S, about 0.6 m resolution, georeferenced
   RGB. Also expect PNG/JPG (non-georeferenced).
2. Accuracy (50%) is scored against an "absolute DSM such as SRTM or
   Copernicus". This is critical: the reference is a ~30 m DSM, NOT LiDAR.
   - For GeoTIFF input, your heights must be absolute and match DEM heights.
   - For PNG/JPG input, relative heights at any scale are acceptable.
3. Visualization (50%): projection accuracy, visual fidelity, flythrough
   navigability, interface intuitiveness, software stability, and standalone
   deployment.
4. Scoring is relative to other teams. There is no pass mark.

Consequences:
- A team that outputs a DSM whose 30 m averages equal Copernicus will score
  very well on RMSE, even with mediocre building detail. Nobody in the
  competitor set explicitly optimises for this. This is our biggest opening.
- Vertical datum mistakes (geoid vs ellipsoid, 50–90 m in India) will destroy
  a team's RMSE. Only one competitor (ChakraVIEW) visibly shows a datum.
- For visualization, judges are SAC scientists. Credible, precise and
  stable beats flashy. Fake-looking numbers will cost trust.

2.1  ArnabTechiee/depthwizard  (shadow geometry; screenshots: "antakya")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they built
- Heights from SHADOW GEOMETRY, not from the depth model:
  h = shadow_length_px × GSD ÷ tan(sun_elevation)  (their formula).
- Depth Anything V2 Base used only for ground mask and building footprints.
  They report DA-V2 Large gave R² = −0.74 on shadow anchors ("worse than the
  mean") and so demoted the network.
- Sun elevation/azimuth read from Maxar metadata.
- Viewer: textured mesh with real vertical walls, height probe ("25.4 m,
  ≈8.5 storeys, 843 m² footprint, ±7.3 m repeatability"), flood simulation
  with "submerged area %" and "buildings affected", orbit/walk, compass,
  vertical exaggeration, mesh stats (608k tris, 41,989 walls).
- FastAPI + Docker Compose, vendored Three.js, HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1, tested with
  network disabled.

Claimed numbers (vs USGS 3DEP LiDAR, per building):
- Fort Myers n=140: RMSE 3.88 m, MAE 3.10 m, bias −0.48 m, r = 0.067
- Punta Gorda n=110: RMSE 7.90 m, r = 0.317
- Repeatability MAE by terrain: urban 3.02, suburban 0.84, hilly 1.54,
  forest 2.30, sparse unmeasurable.

Strengths
- Physically grounded, honest, well documented, strong limitations section.
- Best-looking building walls among the competitors [screenshot].
- Terrain-stratified reporting (exactly what the PS asks for).

Weaknesses (our opportunities)
- Correlation r = 0.067 at Fort Myers means it barely ranks buildings by
  height. RMSE looks OK only because the buildings are similar heights.
- Only building heights where a shadow is measurable: 74% urban, 36% hilly,
  16% forest, ~1% sparse. Everything else is not measured at all.
- They use DIVISION by tan(elevation). Physically, height = shadow length ×
  tan(sun elevation). Division is only correct if the metadata angle is the
  sun ZENITH angle. Their "empirically better" result suggests an angle
  convention issue. We should get this right, and can mention it.
- Terrain is not the focus; the output is not a full-scene absolute DSM
  optimised against Copernicus.
- Walls are voxel/stair-stepped ("jaggy") in close-up [screenshot].
- Uses Maxar, not Cartosat; no DEM fusion for terrain.

What to take / beat
- Take: shadow anchors as ONE calibration source (not the whole method).
- Take: building inspector card (height, storeys, footprint).
- Take: flood "buildings affected" count.
- Beat: full-scene DSM (terrain + every building), correct trig, clean LoD1
  walls, much better correlation, DEM-consistent absolute heights.

PART 5 — ACCURACY CHANGES (50% OF MARKS)
================================================================================

Goal: for GeoTIFF inputs, minimise RMSE against Copernicus/SRTM while
showing real building detail; for PNG/JPG, maximise shape correlation.

A1. Fix the above-ground calibration bug  [P0, ~2 h]
    File: depthwizard/calibrate.py (DEM-fusion branch)
    Problem: structure = rel − gaussian(rel). For an AGL-trained model this
    makes ground negative next to buildings and flattens dense canopy.
    Change:
      - Detect whether the backbone is AGL-trained (store a flag in the
        checkpoint's config.json, e.g. "depthwizard_target": "agl").
      - For AGL models: ground_level = grey_opening(rel, size ≈ 1.5 ×
        largest expected building width in px, e.g. 60 m / GSD), then
        smooth it. structure = max(rel − ground_level, 0).
      - Keep the old high-pass path for the generic model.
    Test: forest_north / forest_south, samples/synthetic. Expect MAE to
    drop below the DEM baseline.

A2. Correct the DEM for what it already contains  [P0, ~3 h]
    SRTM (C-band) and Copernicus GLO-30 (X-band) are surface models: they
    already include part of the canopy and buildings at 30 m. Adding our
    full structure height on top double-counts.
      - DTM_est = DEM − β × block_mean_30m(k × structure)
        where β ≈ 0.5–0.8 (penetration/averaging factor). Fit β per
        landscape class on scenes with a real DTM/DSM reference, or keep
        a table: urban 0.6, forest 0.7, sparse 0.3.
      - DSM = DTM_est + k × structure.

A3. Reference-consistent mode (optimises the actual scoring)  [P0, ~2 h]
    Add a final step so that, for every 30 m DEM cell, the mean of our DSM
    equals the DEM value:
      DSM_final = DSM + upsample( DEM − block_mean_30m(DSM) )
      (use smooth bilinear upsampling of the correction to avoid seams).
    - Make it a user option "Match reference DEM at 30 m" (on by default
      for evaluation).
    - Report both numbers in Validate: native-resolution RMSE and 30 m
      aggregated RMSE.
    - This alone will likely put us ahead of every team scoring against
      Copernicus/SRTM.

A4. Vertical datum handling  [P0, ~2 h]
    - Copernicus GLO-30 heights: EGM2008 geoid. SRTM: EGM96 geoid.
      Cartosat CartoDEM: EGM96. Reference may be ellipsoidal (WGS84).
    - Add a vertical datum field to meta.json and the GeoTIFF tags.
    - Use pyproj with geoid grids (egm96_15.gtx, egm08_25.gtx; bundle them
      so it works offline) to convert when the DEM and requested output
      datum differ.
    - UI: show "Vertical datum: EGM2008" in the inspector; option in the
      upload dialog.
    - In India the geoid–ellipsoid separation is about −50 to −95 m, so a
      mismatch is catastrophic for RMSE.

A5. Shadow anchors as a calibration source  [P1, ~1 day]
    New file: depthwizard/shadows.py
      1. Read sun elevation and azimuth from metadata (Cartosat .META /
         XML, GeoTIFF tags, or a UI field). If missing, estimate azimuth
         from the dominant shadow direction (structure tensor on the
         shadow mask).
      2. Shadow mask: low brightness + low saturation + blue shift in
         LAB; Otsu threshold; morphological clean-up.
      3. Building footprints: connected components of structure > 2 m
         (or segmentation, A8).
      4. For each footprint, march from the footprint edge along the
         anti-sun direction; shadow length L = contiguous shadow pixels.
      5. Height = L × GSD × tan(sun_elevation). Validate the angle
         convention on one scene with known heights; if the metadata
         gives a zenith angle, use cot instead. Log which was used.
      6. Use the anchors (building median structure vs shadow height) in a
         Huber fit to get k. Priority: GCP > shadow anchors > DEM fit >
         scene prior.
    - Show the anchors in the viewer as small markers ("measured from
      shadow"). This is a strong visual story for judges.

A6. Spatially varying scale  [P1, ~4 h]
    One global k is wrong when a scene mixes towers and villages, or
    flat and hilly areas. Fit k (and offset) per 500 m block with robust
    regression, then smooth the k-field. Idea from Sumit's "per-region
    RANSAC".

A7. Test-time augmentation + uncertainty map  [P0, ~3 h]
    File: depthwizard/depth.py
      - Predict on 8 variants (4 rotations × flip), undo the transform,
        align each to the mean (affine), average.
      - Uncertainty = per-pixel standard deviation across variants (and
        across Small+Base if both available).
      - Save uncertainty.tif alongside dsm.tif, show a "Confidence"
        overlay, and report RMSE separately for high- vs low-confidence
        pixels (proves the map is meaningful).
    Expected: TTA typically reduces RMSE by a few percent and removes
    tile artefacts.

A8. Water and shadow cleanup  [P1, ~3 h]
    - Water: blue/dark + very low texture variance (or a simple NDWI proxy
      when NIR exists in Cartosat MX); force structure = 0 and DSM = DTM.
    - Shadow pits: where the shadow mask is on and structure is below the
      local ground, clamp to ground.

A9. Building footprints and flat roofs  [P1, ~1 day]
    - Footprints from: thresholded structure > 2 m + watershed split, or
      a segmentation model (SAM2 with box prompts, or a SegFormer trained
      on building masks). Optional OSM footprints when online.
    - Replace each footprint's heights with its robust roof height
      (70th percentile), optionally with a planar fit for sloped roofs.
    - Gives crisp LoD1 geometry for both accuracy (sharp edges) and
      rendering (walls, Part 6).

A10. Better backbone training  [P1, runs overnight]
    - Resample training tiles to random GSD 0.3–1.0 m so the model
      generalises to Cartosat 0.6 m.
    - Train a METRIC head as well: L1 on AGL in metres (GAMUS gives true
      metres) with an input-GSD normalisation. Then the model itself
      predicts building heights in metres, and k becomes a check rather
      than a guess.
    - Fine-tune DA-V2 Base (fits 8 GB with batch 2, grad accumulation 4,
      AMP, gradient checkpointing). Compare with Small on the 30-tile test.
    - Evaluate Depth Anything 3 (DA3 Mono/Metric) as a backbone — at least
      one competitor uses DA3MONO-LARGE. Keep whichever wins on the test.
    - Augmentations: colour jitter matching Cartosat MX, haze, JPEG,
      slight blur (0.6 m sensor PSF).
    - More data: GAMUS full train set (done), plus ISPRS Vaihingen/Potsdam
      and IEEE DFC 2018/2019 if licences allow.

A11. Tile consistency for big Cartosat scenes  [P2]
    - Current two-pass affine alignment works for relative models. For a
      metric model, align tiles by median offset only (not scale) and
      blend.

A12. PNG/JPG mode improvements  [P1, ~1 h]
    - Relative output 0–1 is fine for scoring, but also offer "estimated
      metres" using the metric head + user GSD, clearly labelled
      "estimated, not calibrated".

PART 6 — 3D RENDERING AND UI CHANGES (50% OF MARKS)
================================================================================

R1. Real buildings (biggest visual upgrade)  [P0, ~1 day]
    - From A9 footprints: simplify polygons (Douglas–Peucker), extrude to
      roof height on top of the DTM.
    - Roof: UV-map the optical image (top-down projection) — roofs look
      exactly like the photo.
    - Walls: neutral material with slight procedural window pattern OR
      sampled colour from the roof edge; ambient occlusion at the base.
    - Terrain mesh uses the DTM (ground only), so no "melted" blobs.
    - Toggle: "Surface (DSM mesh)" vs "City model (LoD1)".
    - Target the look of the Swiss aerial screenshot and Arnab's walls, but
      with clean straight edges instead of stair steps.

R2. GPU terrain and level of detail  [P0, ~4 h]
    - Upload the height grid as a Float32 texture; displace a plane in the
      vertex shader. Vertical exaggeration becomes a uniform (instant,
      no CPU rebuild).
    - Chunked quadtree LOD (e.g. 256×256 chunks, 3 levels) so full
      Cartosat scenes (10k × 10k px) stay at 60 fps. Show "LOD: auto"
      and triangle count in the HUD like amogh.
    - Colour maps (height/slope/error/confidence) computed in the fragment
      shader from textures — no CPU loops on mode switch.

R3. Lighting and visual fidelity  [P0, ~4 h]
    - ACES tone mapping, physically based materials.
    - Directional sun from the real image metadata (sun elevation/azimuth)
      so rendered shadows match the photo. This is a subtle but strong
      "projection accuracy" point.
    - Shadow maps (cascaded for big scenes), SSAO (postprocessing library,
      vendored), subtle bloom off by default.
    - Sky: Three.js Sky shader + aerial-perspective fog. Water: animated
      normal-mapped water plane for the flood simulation (reflective,
      semi-transparent, edges foam) — like the ocean in the flight demo.

R4. Navigation  [P0, ~3 h]
    - Keep Orbit / Fly / Tour. Add:
      - Top-down (orthographic) and "Fit to scene".
      - Walk mode with gravity and collision with building walls.
      - Drone mode: smooth inertia, altitude hold, gamepad support.
      - Click-to-fly: double-click a point → smooth camera travel.
    - Keyframe tour editor: add current view as keyframe, play, export
      MP4/WebM via canvas.captureStream + MediaRecorder. Use this to make
      the demo video.

R5. Layout redesign  [P0, ~1 day]
    Target: professional GIS workstation (amogh-style credibility) with

    - Top bar: project name, "Import", "Validate", "Export", backbone badge

    - Left rail (icons): Scenes, Layers, Analysis, Disaster, Validation,

    - Centre: 3D viewport with floating toolbar (Orbit, Fly, Walk, Top,
      Tour, Fit, Screenshot, Record) and HUD (lon/lat, elevation under
      cursor, slope, camera altitude AGL, FPS, triangles, LOD).
    - Right "Scene inspector":
      - Project state checklist: Input ✓, Depth ✓, Calibration ✓ (method),
        DSM ✓ (absolute/relative), Validation ✓, 3D ✓, Export ✓.
      - Geospatial metadata: CRS, horizontal GSD, vertical datum, bounds
        (lat/lon), sun angles, acquisition date.
      - Calibration card: equation used (DSM = DTM + k·structure),
        k ± error, anchor count (GCP / shadow / DEM), fit R².
      - Analyst cursor: pixel, lon/lat, DSM, DTM, nDSM, slope, aspect,

    - Bottom: north arrow, scale bar, colour legend with P02–P98 range.

This team is much stronger than their README suggested. Their plan is the

  false slopes ("up to 75% of the height range") and give a free
  confidence map. (= our A7 TTA + uncertainty, already done by them.)
- Scale sources: learned prior, one known height, GCPs, shadows, and
  GHSL building heights. "No input needed" for scale.
- COP30 terrain fusion, "metres above sea level".
- Ground/building separation, flat roofs, vertical walls (LoD1).
- GeoTIFF export of DSM, nDSM and DTM.
- Viewer: fly, walk, VR, measure; height/slope/error layers.
- Validation vs LiDAR (AHN4 Netherlands, swissSURFACE3D Switzerland)
  across urban, sparse, hilly and forest; Indian demo scene from
  OpenAerialMap.
- Prototype screenshot shows a validation table with rows like
  "Raw / Datum shift / Robust affine", roughly RMSE 2.85 → 1.94 → 1.43 m
  with r ≈ 0.897, and per-landscape bars around 1.7–2.3 m (small text,
  approximate). Error map, scatter plot and landscape bar chart.
- Stack: React + three.js, FastAPI, PyTorch, OpenCV, GDAL, trimesh,
  Docker, Vite. Claims "runs on a laptop CPU within half a minute".
- References: DA-V2, IM2HEIGHT, Guided Image Filtering, GAMUS, HTC-DC Net.

Their likely weak spots
- Their best numbers are after "datum shift" and "robust affine" fitted
  to the reference itself. Only the "raw" row is a true blind score.
  Our deck should show the blind number prominently and say so.
- LiDAR scenes are European aerial (fine GSD); nothing shown on
  Cartosat-like 0.6 m Indian satellite imagery vs Copernicus.
- No disaster analytics in the deck beyond words (no flood simulation,
  buildings-affected, change detection shown).
- Web app only; no installer mentioned.

What this changes in our plan
1. TTA + confidence (A7) is now table stakes, not novelty. Still do it.
2. ADD A13 — Planar tilt removal [P0, 1 h]: fit and remove a plane (or
   low-order surface) from the network output before calibration; in DEM
   mode the DEM supplies the true slope. Their "fake tilt up to 75% of
   height range" finding is worth guarding against.
3. ADD A14 — GHSL building-height prior [P1, 3 h]: JRC GHS-BUILT-H
   (100 m average building height, global, free) as another scale anchor
   for k in urban areas. Bundle the India tiles for offline use.
4. Our differentiators must now be:
   - 30 m reference-consistent output (A3) + correct vertical datum (A4),
     scored on Indian scenes against Copernicus — the actual judging setup.
   - A blind (no reference fitting) accuracy table with ablation (V3).
   - Real disaster analytics: connected flood, buildings affected,

   - Cartosat-specific handling (sun/view angles from metadata,
     pansharpening) and a tested offline Windows installer (S1, S3).
   - Visual quality: textured LoD1 buildings with sun-matched lighting.
5. Match their validation visuals: error map, predicted-vs-reference
   scatter plot, per-landscape bar chart — in the Validate tab and PDF.
